Pegs, dowels, dovetails or flat cuts

A good joint lines parts up perfectly and holds them while the glue sets. Here's how to choose one.

Printed pegs

With pegs, one side of the cut gets pegs and the other gets matching sockets. They need no extra hardware and line parts up well. The trade-off is that a peg sticks out, so that face can't sit flat on the bed. Bedsplit handles this by keeping each part's biggest cut face peg-free, so it prints flat on that face. Where two neighbouring parts both need the same face, both get holes and a separate printed pin joins them.

Dowel holes

Both sides get blind holes. You join them with printed pins (included in the zip), wooden dowels, or lengths of filament. Every cut face stays flat, which helps with orientation, and wooden dowels make the strongest joint.

Sliding dovetails

One part gets a dovetail rail and its neighbour gets a matching slot. Because the rail's sides flare out at 15°, the parts lock together: they can only come apart by sliding back the way they went in. Bedsplit plans the build order and the direction each part slides in, preferring to drop parts down from above, and checks that nothing blocks the path. The assembly sheet lists the steps.

Where a rail can't slide in, for example on interlocking shapes, both parts get a channel instead and a separate printed double-dovetail key slides in afterwards. You can also choose keys for every seam. Keys print lying on a wide face, and a drop of CA glue makes any dovetail permanent.

Start with a 10 mm wide, 5 mm deep rail and 0.2 mm clearance. If rails are tight, go to 0.25–0.3 mm.

Flat cuts

Flat cuts add no joints at all. They're best for thin walls where a connector won't fit, or when you plan to bolt or plastic-weld the parts yourself.

SettingStarting pointNotes
Diameter8 mmUse 5–6 mm on small faces, 10 mm for big heavy parts.
Length10 mmLonger pegs align better but need thicker walls.
Clearance0.2 mmGo to 0.3 mm if pegs are tight, or 0.1 mm for a press fit on a well-tuned printer.

If a part ends up with no joints, the cut face was too thin for the connector plus wall. Try a smaller diameter, or glue that seam.
