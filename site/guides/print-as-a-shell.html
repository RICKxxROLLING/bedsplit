Printing big models as shells

A 400 mm statue split into solid blocks can use kilograms of filament and days of print time. As a shell with a fixed wall thickness, the same model often needs a fraction of that.

What shell mode does

Tick Hollow into a shell before splitting and set a wall thickness. Bedsplit keeps a solid outer wall of that thickness and removes everything inside, then splits the shell. Each part comes out as a ring, a curved panel or an open cup, not a solid block.

The wall is measured from the real surface, so it stays within a fraction of a millimetre of what you set, even on curved shapes.

Picking a wall thickness

WallGood forPegs that fit
5–6 mmDisplay pieces, cosplay, propsabout 2–3 mm
8 mmMost large models: a good balanceabout 5 mm
10–12 mmParts that get handled, outdoor piecesabout 7 mm, or dovetails

Joints inside the wall

Pegs and dowel holes shrink to fit inside the wall, with at least 1 mm of plastic around every hole. They're placed along the middle of the ring so several pegs spread around each seam. Where the wall curves away from the cut, a peg gets shorter rather than breaking through the surface. Dovetails need a wall of about 9 mm or more.

Slicer settings

  • The wall is already the strength, so a few perimeters plus normal infill works well. With 100% infill the wall prints fully solid.
  • Parts are cut open by the splits, so most have no inside roof. A part with a closed hollow inside, like the very top of a dome, has an inside ceiling. Print it opening-down, or turn on tree supports.
